Labour Markets · Minimum wage

National minimum wage to median wage by departments in Colombia (2025)

Didier Hermida Giraldo

In Colombia the minimum wage (MW) is set nationwide. It is revised every December by a Concertation Committee consisting of representatives from the national government, unions and business associations and it applies to all occupations, industries and regions. Thus, the same minimum wage applies to Bogotá (the capital, the country’s main economic engine, and the region with the lowest informality rate) and to Chocó (the poorest region and one of those with the highest informality rates).

The ratio of the MW to the median wage tells us how much pressure minimum-wage policy places on salaried employment. The map plots this ratio. Bogotá has the lowest value, 0,7. This means that the MW equals 70% of the median salaried worker’s earnings. At the other extreme are Chocó and Nariño, with ratios of about 0,94. For comparison, in Europe the ratio is around 0,51¹. Overall, Colombia shows a center-periphery pattern; the lowest ratios sit in the central regions while the highest are found in the periphery.

Some argue that a higher MW to the median wage ratio is associated with informality. The reasoning is that when the MW is set too high, some salaried workers (those in the left tail of the wage distribution) would otherwise be paid below it. If employers’ only options were to hire these workers at the legal wage or not at all, the result could be unemployment. Nevertheless, in countries like Colombia, and specially in regions with week institutions, there is another margin of adjustment: informality. Therefore, employers can hire workers informally at wages below the legal minimum. In fact, the map of informality by departments correlates with the map of the MW to the median wage ratio: the departments with the highest ratios also have the highest informality rates (the coefficient of correlation between this two measures is 0,79).

A note on map coverage: unfortunately, the map does not display the Archipelago of San Andrés, Providencia and Santa Catalina. Although it is part of Colombia, the base map provided by Datawrapper does not include it. Data are also unavailable for Arauca, Casanare, Putumayo, Amazonas, Guainía, Guaviare, Vaupés, and Vichada.
